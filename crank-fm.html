Crank FM

Internet radio for the Playdate, tuned with the crank like an old tabletop radio. Turn the dial and you hear static; come close to a station and its music breaks through, muffled and wavering, until it settles in clear as the needle reaches it.

What it does

A dial from 88 to 108, with seven bands: pop, rock, jazz, classic, news, chill and rap. The stations are real internet radio from around the world.

The crank turns the dial; a fast spin keeps it turning, and it settles onto the nearest station. ⬅️ and ➡️ jump to the next station, Ⓑ changes band, ⬆️ and ⬇️ set the volume. Ⓐ saves the station you are on, and your saved stations get their own heart key.

The system menu has region (pick a country, or a language such as Arabic, Japanese or Hindi), dark mode, and help.

The station's name, the song playing (in Arabic too), where the station is from, signal bars and how long you have been listening.

Real stereo in headphones; on the speaker, a mono mix at the same loudness.

How to put it on your Playdate

  1. Download CrankFM-Playdate.zip above and unzip it, so you have CrankFM.pdx.
  2. Go to play.date/account/sideload, sign in, and upload CrankFM.pdx.
  3. On the Playdate, open Settings → Games and refresh: Crank FM appears in your library. It needs Wi-Fi to play.

How it works

It is written in C, because the Playdate's simpler Lua tools cannot play sound from the internet. The app streams each station as MP3 over Wi-Fi and decodes it on the Playdate itself. The static, the muffling and the warble are mixed in live, depending on how far the dial is from the station, so finding a station sounds the way it does on a real radio.

The Playdate's Wi-Fi is only just fast enough for a 128 kbps stream, so the app keeps a buffer of a few seconds, and makes it longer by itself if the signal gets weak. A limiter keeps loud songs from crackling on the small speaker.

Where the stations come from

Crank FM finds its stations in Radio Browser, a free, community-run directory of internet radio with an open API. It asks for stations the Playdate can play, sorts them into the seven bands, and keeps the list for a week. If a station turns out to be off the air, a backup takes its place.

Crank FM does not host any audio: every stream belongs to its station. The first version played only SomaFM, but their terms do not allow third-party apps, so that version stays on my own Playdate, and SomaFM is left out of this one on purpose. If you like their stations, listen at somafm.com and support them if you can.

The source code

The code is free for anyone who wants it (MIT licence): the whole radio, from the dial and the static to the decoder, the buffer, the station directory and the sound. The README explains what is where and how to build it.
